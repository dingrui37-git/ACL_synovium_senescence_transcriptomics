# Manuscript-ready Methods text

## Directional-score matched-gene-set negative control

To test whether the mouse-derived directional score was specific to the projected signature rather than a generic property of the pig injury transcriptome, we generated 10,000 matched random gene sets independently for the early and chronic pig datasets. The fixed signature contained 75 measurable one-to-one pig orthologues, comprising 65 genes assigned to the up component and 10 to the down component. Candidate controls were restricted to measured pig genes satisfying the same pig-side one-to-one pig-to-human orthologue eligibility criterion used for projected-signature construction, after exclusion of the 75 signature genes. Gene-level variability was calculated as the standard deviation across all samples in the corresponding cohort. Controls were jointly stratified into five quantile bins for mean TMM-normalized logCPM and five bins for variability. Within each mean-expression × variability stratum, each random set sampled unique genes without replacement and preserved the observed numbers assigned to the up and down components; genes could recur across independent permutations. Random sets were scored using the same gene-wise z standardization and directional-score formula as the observed signature. For each prespecified pig comparison, the statistic was the case-minus-control difference in median directional score. One-sided empirical P values were calculated as (1 + number of null statistics greater than or equal to the observed statistic)/(10,000 + 1), with the positive direction prespecified as injury-concordant; two-sided empirical tests were evaluated as a sensitivity analysis. Benjamini–Hochberg correction was applied across the three pig comparisons. Random seeds were fixed separately for the early and chronic cohorts (20260901 and 20260902, respectively).

## Senescence-focused pathway/gene-set enrichment

For each of the five prespecified injury contrasts, genes were ranked using the predefined statistic sign(logFC) × −log10(P value used for ranking) from the frozen rank files and analyzed with the fgsea multilevel procedure (minSize = 5, maxSize = 500, eps = 0). The prespecified eight-set panel comprised five direct senescence/SASP signatures—REACTOME_CELLULAR_SENESCENCE, REACTOME_SENESCENCE_ASSOCIATED_SECRETORY_PHENOTYPE_SASP, FRIDMAN_SENESCENCE_UP, FRIDMAN_SENESCENCE_DN and SenMayo—and three mechanistically related contextual readouts—HALLMARK_P53_PATHWAY, HALLMARK_E2F_TARGETS and HALLMARK_G2M_CHECKPOINT. The latter were not interpreted as stand-alone evidence of senescence. Reactome, Fridman and Hallmark sets were obtained through msigdbr for the ranked species using the package's species-specific human-MSigDB orthologue projection; gene symbols were deduplicated before testing. SenMayo used the authors' original murine list directly for mouse, whereas the published human list was mapped to Sus scrofa using g:Profiler; unique mapped pig symbols and the complete mapping audit were retained. The lower minimum overlap threshold of five genes was used to retain the prespecified FRIDMAN_SENESCENCE_DN set, which contains 10 source genes and 8–9 represented genes in the frozen rank vectors; the full testability audit is provided in supplementary source data. These eight entries were adjusted by Benjamini–Hochberg within each comparison as one prespecified targeted family. The two contextual readouts, HALLMARK_INFLAMMATORY_RESPONSE and HALLMARK_EPITHELIAL_MESENCHYMAL_TRANSITION, were not reselected into this eight-set family; their FDR values were taken directly from the original complete 50-set Hallmark analyses for each comparison. The Figure 6 directional-score row is a separate analysis: the mouse values are descriptive derivation-species reference scores calculated from the 75 mouse genes corresponding to the measurable one-to-one pig orthologues and their preassigned directions. Because these genes and directions were derived from the mouse data, mouse score P values are not treated as independent validation evidence and no inferential FDR is assigned to the mouse cells. Pig values are the projected 75-orthologue validation scores analyzed with the prespecified rank-sum tests, while retaining the original dataset-specific primary score-analysis BH correction: all 12 score-comparison rows for the early pig analysis (six score definitions across two time contrasts) and all six score definitions for the chronic pig analysis. Both species use the same signed, gene-wise z-standardized score formula.

## Reactome/KEGG broader pathway analysis

To provide complementary biological context for the direction-consistent persistent genes, over-representation analysis was performed separately for the persistent-up (n = 970) and persistent-down (n = 446) gene lists. The common eligibility universe comprised 14,184 unique mouse Entrez genes that passed the relevant expression filtering, were formally tested at both 1 and 4 weeks, and had valid identifiers. For each database, the hypergeometric universe was defined as the intersection of this common eligibility universe with genes represented in that database (7,327 Reactome genes and 6,421 KEGG genes); the same database-specific annotated universe was used for both persistent-up and persistent-down analyses, and no direction-specific background was constructed. After restriction to pathways containing 10–500 genes in the corresponding annotated universe, 937 Reactome and 350 KEGG pathways were retained for testing. Enrichment was assessed with the one-sided hypergeometric upper-tail test implemented by `stats::phyper` in R 4.5.2. Gene identifiers were Entrez IDs; duplicate gene–pathway mappings were deduplicated before testing. Reactome definitions were obtained from mouse-native MSigDB M2:CP:REACTOME (release 2026.1.Mm), and KEGG pathways were retrieved for Mus musculus through KEGGREST (snapshot dated 31 August 2026). For each direction, Benjamini–Hochberg correction was applied jointly across all 1,287 Reactome and KEGG pathway tests. The supplementary dot plot displays up to eight pathways with the lowest FDR among FDR-supported pathways in each database-by-direction stratum; strata with no FDR-supported pathways would have no points displayed. The complete results for all tested pathways, including nominal P values, BH-adjusted FDR values, pathway sizes, overlap counts and overlap genes, are provided as source data.

## Concise legend notes

Asterisks indicate BH-adjusted FDR < 0.05 within the applicable inferential testing family. In the matched-null figure, the shaded density is the distribution of 10,000 expression- and variability-matched random-gene-set effects, dashed lines mark the null 95% interval, and the red line marks the observed signature effect. In Figure 6, heatmap colour denotes the signed directional-score difference or NES. The five direct and three mechanistic panel rows use BH across eight entries within each comparison, whereas inflammation and EMT retain FDR from the complete 50-set Hallmark analyses. Mouse directional-score cells are descriptive and have no inferential FDR; pig score cells retain the original early-pig and chronic-pig primary score-analysis BH-FDR families.
