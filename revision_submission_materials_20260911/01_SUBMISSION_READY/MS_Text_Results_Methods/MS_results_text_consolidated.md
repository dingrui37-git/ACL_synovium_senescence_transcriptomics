# Results text for the added analyses

## Figure 6: senescence-focused enrichment

The supporting readouts showed a temporal rather than uniform pattern. E2F and G2M enrichment peaked at 1 week in both species and declined at later time points. This trajectory was consistent with attenuation of an early injury-associated proliferative/cell-cycle transcriptional response, although it did not by itself demonstrate stable cell-cycle arrest or senescence. The p53 trajectory differed by species: NES increased modestly in mouse, whereas it was negative in early pig and became positive at 52 weeks. The negative early-pig p53 pattern reflected a mixed transcriptional response, with negatively ranked members such as COQ8A, RETSAT, SESN1 and CEBPA despite positive contributions from canonical stress-associated genes including TP53, BAX, GADD45A, GADD45B and CDKN1A (see Supplementary Data 7). None of the p53 enrichments survived panel-level FDR correction.

Neither FRIDMAN_SENESCENCE_UP nor FRIDMAN_SENESCENCE_DN survived panel-level FDR correction. FRIDMAN_SENESCENCE_UP was positively enriched in the mouse cohorts and chronic pig synovium, with leading edges containing extracellular-matrix and senescence-associated genes such as SPARC, COL1A2, FN1, THBS1, SERPINE1 and CDKN1A/CDKN2A, whereas early pig contrasts showed negative NES values driven largely by GSN, IGFBP5, CREG1, CITED2 and F3. FRIDMAN_SENESCENCE_DN showed positive NES values in all contrasts but was dominated by a very small eight- to nine-gene overlap, particularly CCNB1, COL3A1, CDC25B and E2F4. These signatures therefore showed context-dependent, non-concordant enrichment rather than definitive evidence for or against senescence (Supplementary Data 8).

## Supplementary Figure 5: matched-gene-set null control

Observed injury-associated directional-score differences were 0.970, 0.691 and 0.302 for early-pig 1 week, early-pig 4 weeks and chronic-pig 52 weeks, respectively. All three exceeded the corresponding matched-gene-set null distributions in 10,000 permutations. The null model therefore supported specificity of the projected signature beyond matched random gene sets.

## Supplementary Figure 6: Reactome/KEGG broader context

Complementary Reactome and KEGG over-representation analyses provided broader pathway context for the persistent-up and persistent-down programs. Sixty-nine persistent-up and 32 persistent-down pathway records were supported at the joint FDR threshold; complete results for all tested pathways are provided in Supplementary Data 6.

## Supplementary Table 1: sex-adjusted sensitivity

Adjustment for sex had minimal effects on the estimated treatment-associated transcriptional response. Primary and sex-adjusted logFC values were highly correlated at 1 week (Pearson r = 0.9996) and 4 weeks (r = 0.9990), with 98.95% and 97.90% of primary strict DEGs retained, respectively. The persistent program retained 1,384 of 1,416 genes (97.74%) after sex adjustment.

## Supplementary Table 2: temporal components

Compared with the persistent program, the 1-week-specific response was more strongly characterized by acute immune, mitochondrial-respiratory and oxidative-phosphorylation processes, whereas the smaller 4-week-specific component did not show FDR-supported Reactome or KEGG pathways.

The original Hallmark inflammatory-response and epithelial–mesenchymal transition GSEA results remain in Figures 3–5, with FDR values derived from the complete Hallmark analyses used for those figures.
