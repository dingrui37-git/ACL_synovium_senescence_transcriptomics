## Directional-score matched-gene-set negative control

To assess whether the mouse-defined directional signature was specific to the injury-associated response rather than a generic property of the pig transcriptome, we compared the observed directional-score effect with 10,000 random gene sets in each pig dataset matched for gene-set size, the 65:10 up/down composition, mean expression and across-sample variability. Control genes were restricted to measured pig genes satisfying the same pig-side one-to-one pig-to-human orthologue eligibility criterion used for projected-signature construction. In the early pig cohort, the observed median case–control score differences were 0.970 at 1 week and 0.691 at 4 weeks, whereas the corresponding 95% matched-null intervals were −0.258 to 0.382 and −0.357 to 0.260. Both one-sided empirical P values were 1.0 × 10−4, with BH-adjusted FDR = 1.5 × 10−4 for both comparisons. In chronic pig synovium at 52 weeks, the observed difference was 0.302, compared with a null interval of −0.154 to 0.219 (empirical P = 0.00290; BH-FDR = 0.00290). Two-sided empirical tests gave the same qualitative conclusion (BH-FDR ≤ 0.00330). Thus, the projected signature produced effects beyond matched null distributions in all three pig contrasts, supporting specificity of the cross-species directional signal [Fig. X; Supplementary Fig. X].

## Senescence-focused pathway and gene-set enrichment

The prespecified eight-set panel yielded 14 FDR-supported results among 40 pathway-by-comparison tests, with BH correction applied across the eight entries within each comparison. In mouse synovium, the SenMayo signature was positively enriched at both 1 week (NES = 2.22, FDR = 5.46 × 10−8) and 4 weeks (NES = 2.07, FDR = 2.27 × 10−6). In pig synovium, REACTOME_CELLULAR_SENESCENCE was positively enriched at early 1 week (NES = 1.82, FDR = 3.31 × 10−5), early 4 weeks (NES = 1.43, FDR = 0.0176) and chronic 52 weeks (NES = 1.50, FDR = 0.0202). REACTOME_SENESCENCE_ASSOCIATED_SECRETORY_PHENOTYPE_SASP was positively enriched at early 1 week (NES = 1.51, FDR = 0.0231) and chronic 52 weeks (NES = 1.59, FDR = 0.0202). Neither FRIDMAN_SENESCENCE_UP nor FRIDMAN_SENESCENCE_DN passed panel-level FDR correction. The FRIDMAN_SENESCENCE_UP pattern was positive in the mouse cohorts and chronic pig synovium, where the leading edge was enriched for extracellular-matrix and senescence-associated genes such as SPARC, COL1A2, FN1, THBS1, SERPINE1 and CDKN1A/CDKN2A. In contrast, the early pig contrasts showed negative NES values, driven largely by negatively ranked genes including GSN, IGFBP5, CREG1, CITED2 and F3. FRIDMAN_SENESCENCE_DN yielded positive NES values across all contrasts, but this result was dominated by a very small cross-species overlap of eight to nine genes, particularly CCNB1, COL3A1, CDC25B and E2F4. Together, these findings indicate that the Fridman signatures are not uniformly conserved across species and time points and should be interpreted as context-dependent, non-concordant enrichment rather than definitive evidence for or against senescence (Supplementary Data 8).

The supporting mechanistic readouts showed a distinct pattern. HALLMARK_P53_PATHWAY was not significant after panel-level correction. HALLMARK_E2F_TARGETS was positively enriched in mouse at 1 week and in the early pig cohort at 1 and 4 weeks, whereas HALLMARK_G2M_CHECKPOINT was positively enriched in mouse at 1 week and in pig at early 1 week, early 4 weeks and chronic 52 weeks. These results did not support a uniform cell-cycle-arrest pattern across species and time points. The pre-existing Hallmark contextual readouts retained FDR values from the complete 50-set Hallmark analyses: epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) was significant in all five contrasts, whereas inflammatory response was significant in mouse at both time points and in pig at early 1 week only. The separate Figure 6 directional-score summary showed positive projected-score differences in all pig contrasts (0.970, 0.691 and 0.302), with the corresponding primary score-analysis BH-FDR values of 0.00507, 0.00507 and 0.00402; mouse scores were displayed as descriptive derivation-species references without inferential FDR. Overall, the panel supported a heterogeneous, species- and time-dependent senescence/SASP-associated remodeling pattern rather than a single invariant senescence program [Fig. X].

## Reactome/KEGG broader pathway analysis

The direction-consistent persistent response comprised 1,416 mouse genes, including 970 persistently upregulated and 446 persistently downregulated genes. Direction-stratified Reactome and KEGG over-representation analysis identified 69 FDR-supported pathway records for the persistent-up list and 32 for the persistent-down list [Supplementary Fig. X; Supplementary Table X].

Persistent-up genes were strongly concentrated in extracellular-matrix synthesis and turnover. Reactome extracellular matrix organization included 89 of 225 pathway genes (enrichment ratio = 5.76, FDR = 1.13 × 10−43), collagen formation included 36 of 71 genes (enrichment ratio = 7.39, FDR = 5.93 × 10−21), and extracellular-matrix degradation included 38 of 81 genes (enrichment ratio = 6.83, FDR = 8.07 × 10−21). Concordant KEGG enrichments included ECM–receptor interaction (26/80 genes; enrichment ratio = 4.41; FDR = 3.25 × 10−9), focal adhesion (32/191; enrichment ratio = 2.27; FDR = 3.43 × 10−4) and cytokine–cytokine receptor interaction (32/173; enrichment ratio = 2.51; FDR = 4.71 × 10−5). Reactome neutrophil degranulation and hemostasis were also supported (FDR = 0.026 and 0.030, respectively).

Persistent-down genes were enriched for metabolic and cytoprotective programs. KEGG PPAR signaling included 19 of 62 genes (enrichment ratio = 7.72, FDR = 1.47 × 10−9), and AMPK signaling included 18 of 115 genes (enrichment ratio = 3.94, FDR = 1.28 × 10−4). Reactome metabolism of lipids included 41 of 496 genes (enrichment ratio = 2.60, FDR = 6.21 × 10−6), while cytoprotection by HMOX1 included 9 of 43 genes (enrichment ratio = 6.58, FDR = 1.01 × 10−3). Most persistent effects were quantitatively attenuated at 4 weeks: only 205 of 970 persistent-up genes and 43 of 446 persistent-down genes showed a larger absolute effect at 4 weeks than at 1 week. Together, these analyses identified coordinated matrix remodeling, adhesion and inflammatory signaling among persistent-up genes, alongside reduced metabolic and cytoprotective programs among persistent-down genes. These enrichments describe molecular processes represented among the persistent genes and do not establish pathway-level causality [Supplementary Fig. X; Supplementary Table X].
