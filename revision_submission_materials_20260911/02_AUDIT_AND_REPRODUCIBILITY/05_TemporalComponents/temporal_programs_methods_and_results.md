# Supplementary temporal-program analysis

## Suggested supplementary-table title

**Supplementary Table X | Representative genes and Reactome/KEGG pathway context for persistent and time-specific mouse injury programs.**

## Methods text for the manuscript

Mouse strict DEGs were defined using the locked Step07 criteria (FDR < 0.05 and |logFC| > 1). The persistent program comprised genes meeting these criteria at both 1 and 4 weeks with the same logFC direction. The 1-week-only and 4-week-only programs comprised genes meeting the criteria at only the indicated time point. Within each temporal class, up- and down-regulated genes were analyzed separately by one-sided hypergeometric over-representation analysis. The background comprised the 14,184 genes that were eligible for testing at both time points and had valid identifiers; for each database, this was intersected with the annotated mouse gene universe (7,327 Reactome genes and 6,421 KEGG genes). Reactome and KEGG pathways containing 10–500 genes in the corresponding annotated universe were tested (937 and 350 pathways, respectively). Nominal P values were adjusted by Benjamini–Hochberg jointly across the 1,287 Reactome and KEGG tests within each temporal-class × direction list. Representative genes were the top ten genes per class and direction ranked by −log10(FDR) × |logFC| (averaged across 1 and 4 weeks for persistent genes). The supplementary table reports the complete tested ORA results and displays up to five FDR-supported pathways per database and direction; when no pathway survived correction, the top nominal pathways are shown and explicitly labeled as nominal-only.

## Results text for the manuscript

The persistent program was dominated by extracellular-matrix and collagen remodeling among up-regulated genes, whereas its down-regulated component was enriched for lipid and amino-acid metabolic processes. The 1-week-only program showed a distinct acute-response pattern, with up-regulated genes enriched for neutrophil degranulation, cytokine-receptor/infection-related signaling and neutrophil extracellular-trap-associated pathways, together with down-regulation of aerobic respiration, respiratory electron transport, complex-I, tricarboxylic-acid-cycle and oxidative-phosphorylation pathways. The 4-week-only component was smaller (295 genes; 211 up-regulated and 84 down-regulated) and yielded no Reactome or KEGG pathway that survived pooled BH correction; its nominal signals, including developmental/ECM and complement/coagulation-related terms among up-regulated genes, were therefore treated as exploratory.

## Requested comparative sentence

> Compared with the persistent program, the 1-week-specific response was more strongly characterized by acute innate inflammatory and neutrophil-associated programs, together with suppression of mitochondrial respiration and oxidative phosphorylation, whereas the smaller 4-week-specific component showed no FDR-supported Reactome or KEGG pathway and only weak, nominal signals involving developmental/ECM and complement/coagulation-related terms.

## Interpretation note

The pathway labels are database annotations rather than claims that the tissue is undergoing the named disease processes (for example, “Staphylococcus aureus infection” or “Parkinson disease”). These terms reflect shared molecular genes and should be paraphrased as innate immune, cytokine, mitochondrial or metabolic processes in the main text.
